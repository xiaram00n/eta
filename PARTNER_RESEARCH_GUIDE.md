# Partner research guide — internal, not an operator landing page

## Before asking anyone
Agree his role, approximate hours, expenses and who can access responses.
Participation and introductions are voluntary. Do not promise ownership or pay
that has not been agreed. Do not contact anyone who declined follow-up consent.
Keep notes without guest identities; separate voluntary contact details.

## Start with one real booking
'Walk me through a recent booking, from the first request until you were paid.'
Ask who did each step, the tools used, what the guest needed, and what worked.
Then: 'Was anything difficult about that one?' Accept 'no.' Ask to see a blank
schedule or anonymized workflow only with permission; never collect credentials.

## Confirm the economics, without demanding a ledger
For one service at a time, confirm the reporting unit: one vehicle trip, a tour,
a guest-group stay, event or hourly booking. Is the quoted price per person,
vehicle, hour or event? Are multiple legs included? How many vehicles and hours?
Ask completed units, collected receipts, unpaid balances, refunds and money
passed to other operators separately. Distinguish gross guest payment from the
operator's retained receipts. Identify shared versus service-specific costs.
For each significant cost, record amount/range, period, who pays and whether fixed
or linked to trips. Do not infer wages, net earnings or profit from gross receipts.
Ask what they currently pay for booking tools, phone, dispatch and referral work.
Seasonality: compare a busy and quiet period if they can estimate comfortably.

## Missing work is not one category
Ask about a recent uncompleted request. Did they want it? Was it profitable at
the offered price? No capacity, unsuitable vehicle, too little notice, access,
response delay, price disagreement, guest choice or cancellation? Could software
reasonably have changed the outcome? Avoid counting every inquiry as recoverable.

## Capacity and guest experience
Available seats versus luggage; vehicle types; accessible needs; working hours;
standby versus dedicated driver; travel/positioning; group vehicles; simultaneous
bookings. Ask what guests misunderstand or need reassurance about. Confirm what
must happen before a request is called 'confirmed.' Ask who handles changes,
late arrivals, no-shows, failed payments and unavailable drivers.

## Existing network
Who organizes the radio/group/dispatcher? How are jobs offered, accepted and
handed off? What fees, queues, pickup areas and relationships matter? What works
well? Do not frame informal collaboration as a defect to replace.

## Rules: separate three kinds of answer
A. Reported legal/licensing/fare/insurance/access requirements.
B. Employer, property, dispatcher or association agreements.
C. Driver-community etiquette and relationship practices.
Record the person's understanding and the authority/organization to consult.
Verify A with current authoritative sources and B with the relevant agreement.
Do not publish reported practices as law, declare compliance or request license
images through this form. Confirm current USVI requirements before live operations.

## Pricing research — after a relevant demonstration
Ask what they use now, what it costs and what replacing it would involve.
Show one specific workflow, not a list of hypothetical benefits. Ask what would
prevent use, who can authorize payment, and how much evidence they need.
Then test an explicit price and charging basis, chosen after cost research.
Record yes/no/needs-proof, reason and conditions. Do not invent local price bands.
A stated preference is weaker evidence than a paid pilot or actual purchasing
behavior; never imply an exploratory conversation is a commitment.

## Define a pilot only after selecting the problem
Choose a narrow workflow and baseline: response time, time arranging a booking,
confirmed/completed requests, unpaid balances or guest confusion. Include setup,
support and error handling in costs. Agree operator control, data access, stop
conditions, charges, cancellation and who handles failures. No pilot begins just
because someone ticked 'yes.' Compare outcomes without claiming causality from a
small uncontrolled sample.

## Analysis worksheet
Segment by arrangement, business scope, service, fleet size, demand period and
booking source. Code observed events separately from opinions and assumptions.
Preserve unanswered, zero, not sure and prefer-not-to-say as different states.
Rank candidate work by demonstrated frequency, consequence, operator value,
feasibility and compatibility with existing practice. Record contradictory cases.
Keep an explicit list: learned / uncertain / next evidence needed.
