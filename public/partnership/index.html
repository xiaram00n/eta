ETA is a proposed booking and trip-coordination service for St. Thomas transportation businesses. Guests request rides. Participating operators review the details, decide whether to accept, and coordinate the driver, vehicle and pickup.

The guest prototype shows the traveler’s side. The operator tools are still a proposal. This walkthrough shows the work they may need to support and the questions we must answer before building them.

01 / What sits behind the screen

A website collects the request.
People make the ride possible.

ETA is not just a website. The proposed service connects guest requests with the operator’s decisions and the driver and vehicle committed to do the work.

Illustrative ride / not a live request

A clear request becomes a clear arrangement.

  1. 01

    The guest asks

    Pickup, destination, time, guests and luggage. Enough information to know what the job needs.

    A request, not a confirmed ride
  2. 02

    An operator checks

    A participating operator checks pickup eligibility, vehicle fit, availability and the applicable price before accepting the job.

    A person commits to the work
  3. 03

    The guest knows the plan

    Who is meeting them, where, when, the agreed price, and how to ask for help if something changes.

    A confirmed arrangement
  4. 04

    The ride happens

    Pickup updates, the trip, payment and any follow-up need to match what was agreed.

    A service delivered

The screen helps both sides keep track. The operator's commitment is what makes the ride possible.

Illustrative exception / not a live request

A payment button cannot supply a missing driver.

  1. 01

    The guest asks

    The same clear request arrives. The website can collect it, but that is only the first step.

    Request received
  2. 02

    Nobody can accept

    No participating operator can meet the time or vehicle needs. Interest in the job is not the same as availability.

    Not confirmed
  3. 03

    Give an honest answer

    Ask whether another time would work, or explain that the request cannot be fulfilled. Do not promise a ride or take payment as though it is secured.

    Alternative or clear decline
  4. 04

    Learn from the gap

    Was the issue timing, vehicle capacity, coverage, or the way the request reached operators? That helps us choose what to improve.

    A question to investigate

This is why operators need to participate before we can promise the service to guests.

Proposed operator-side tools · not built

The operator can see what needs a decision.

A useful system would help the person responsible make a real commitment, not show availability that has not been checked.

  1. 01Review the request

    Pickup, destination, time, group, luggage and any service conditions.

  2. 02Decide whether it fits

    Check eligibility, price, driver, vehicle and actual availability. Accept or decline.

  3. 03Record the commitment

    Show who is responsible, what was agreed and what the guest should expect.

  4. 04Handle a change

    Keep the original arrangement clear until an authorized person agrees to a change.

02 / The intended business customer

Keep control of your work.
Give useful requests a clearer path.

ETA is being explored for transportation operators and businesses. It should earn a place in their working day by addressing a real task or cost, not by adding another inbox.

The direction we want to protect

Your business remains yours.

  • Your business name and existing customer relationships.
  • A say in which work you accept and the price you agree.
  • Useful relationships with other drivers, associations and referral partners.
  • The option to build around methods that already work, rather than replace them all at once.
Improvements worth investigating

Less uncertainty. Better opportunities.

  • Requests with the details needed to judge the job.
  • Fewer calls just to establish who is available or where a pickup stands.
  • A clearer way to arrange return rides, groups and work shared with other operators.
  • A useful presence in front of guests who are looking for the service you actually provide.

These are goals to test, not results we can promise. The questionnaire asks which problems happen in your business and what—if anything—a system should change.

Participation has to mean something practical

Choose which work you take. Keep availability accurate enough to rely on. Accept or decline requests. Update important changes. We need to find the simplest way to do that during a real working day.

Existing customers and referral relationships matter. We need to understand when operators want to share work, who can commit a driver and vehicle, and what should stay under their own control.

03 / A reason for guests to use it

Being online is a start.
Being useful is the reason to return.

We need both sides: operators who can deliver the service, and guests who have a reason to find and choose it.

FIND / CHOOSE / RETURN

Which guest?
Which need?
Why this service?

One clearly defined problem is a better starting point than trying to offer everything to every visitor.

  1. Find it

    Give the right people a way to discover it.

    Search and business listings, accommodation referrals, existing contacts, or carefully tested advertising are possible routes. A Google Business Profile is one option to investigate where eligible, not a guarantee of inquiries.

  2. Choose it

    Make the benefit easy to understand.

    A local ride, an arrival pickup, or a return already arranged. Start with a need operators can reliably meet, and explain what is clearer or easier than the alternatives.

  3. Use it again

    Deliver what the guest was told to expect.

    Accurate information and a dependable ride give the guest a reason to come back or recommend it. The website alone cannot create that experience.

These are routes to test, not a completed marketing plan. Advertising costs, demand, competing services and the effort needed to win a booking still need checking.

04 / Why ETA?

A familiar name.
A useful double meaning.

The same three letters connect the guest's journey with the work that makes it happen.

For operators

ETA. = Enhanced Transportation Administrators

A name for the purpose behind the product: supporting the people who organize transportation. Clearer requests, easier coordination, and less duplicated administration around the work they already do.

For guests

ETA. = Estimated Time of Arrival

Familiar transportation language, connected to a practical question: when will my ride arrive? It gives the name a recognizable link to pickups, departures, and getting where you need to be.

ETA is our working name for this prototype. The idea is to make the connection easy to understand; final branding still needs its own review.

05 / Who uses it—and who decides

Different roles.
Different decisions.

Guests use the booking experience. Transportation businesses are the intended customers for any operator tools. Drivers do the work. The exact buyer and operating responsibilities still need to be established.

Guest · booking user

Describe the ride.
Review the arrangement.

Share where and when you need transportation, who is traveling, and any relevant trip details. A request is not a confirmed ride until an operator accepts and the work is committed.

Operator or business · intended customer

Choose the work.
Commit the service.

Decide which requests fit, what price applies, and who will provide the suitable vehicle. Keep control of existing guests and choose when—if ever—to share work.

Driver · service provider

Take the trip
you have agreed to do.

A driver needs to know the pickup, schedule and vehicle arrangement—and to be actually available and committed. The prototype does not assume a location dot creates availability.

06 / What research must settle

Research tells us
what is worth building.

The questionnaire is part of product development. It asks about actual bookings, working methods, existing tools and costs so we can define a useful service rather than guess.

The operator questionnaire answers

What is happening today?

Which bookings you complete, what you collect, what you spend, how guests reach you, what tools you already use, and who can commit a driver and vehicle.

These answers help identify a task or existing expense the system might improve. They do not prove that ETA caused revenue or savings. Estimates are welcome; private records are not needed.

Still to verify before a real pilot

Who can do what—and under which conditions?

Which operators and drivers choose to participate, who has authority to accept and assign work, what pickups and services they can provide, and who handles changes or failed arrangements.

Pickup access, service rules, applicable fares and any separate platform charges need confirmation with the appropriate parties. We also need to identify the first customer, the specific problem to solve and how this would improve on existing options.

  1. Listen

    Walk through a recent booking and hear from willing operators with different ways of working.

  2. Choose

    Pick one recurring problem, a specific guest need, and a small group able to try a different approach.

  3. Prepare

    Confirm pickup eligibility, driver commitments, applicable fares and who handles changes before trying a real trip.

  4. Test & review

    Compare completed work, failures, effort and return with the current approach. Identify what was replaced or improved, not just what the app added.
