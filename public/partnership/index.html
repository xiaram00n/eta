01 / What sits behind the screen

A guest can ask online.
A driver still has to take them there.

The website is the front door. The service is everything that makes the request become a dependable ride.

Illustrative ride / not a live request

A clear request becomes a clear arrangement.

  1. 01

    The guest asks

    Pickup, destination, time, guests and luggage. Enough information to know what the job needs.

    A request, not a confirmed ride
  2. 02

    An operator checks

    A participating operator checks pickup eligibility, vehicle fit, availability and the applicable price before accepting the job.

    A person commits to the work
  3. 03

    Who is meeting them, where, when, the agreed price, and how to ask for help if something changes.

  4. 04

    A service delivered

Illustrative exception / not a live request

A payment button cannot supply a missing driver.

  1. 01

    The guest asks

    The same clear request arrives. The website can collect it, but that is only the first step.

    Request received
  2. 02

    No participating operator can meet the time or vehicle needs. Interest in the job is not the same as availability.

    Not confirmed
  3. 03

  4. 04

    Was the issue timing, vehicle capacity, coverage, or the way the request reached operators? That helps us choose what to improve.

02 / A reason to participate

Keep what works.
Make the difficult parts easier.

We should not ask operators to change their routine just because a newer system exists. The change needs to solve something that matters to them.

The direction we want to protect

Your business remains yours.

  • Your business name and existing customer relationships.
  • A say in which work you accept and the price you agree.
  • Useful relationships with other drivers, associations and referral partners.
  • The option to build around methods that already work, rather than replace them all at once.
Improvements worth investigating

Less uncertainty. Better opportunities.

  • Requests with the details needed to judge the job.
  • Fewer calls just to establish who is available or where a pickup stands.
  • A clearer way to arrange return rides, groups and work shared with other operators.
  • A useful presence in front of guests who are looking for the service you actually provide.

These are design goals, not verified findings or promised results. We need operators to tell us which problems they actually have, and which of these changes would help.

Participation has to mean something practical

Choose which work you take. Keep availability accurate enough to rely on. Accept or decline requests. Update important changes. We need to find the simplest way to do that during a real working day.

Existing customers and referral relationships matter. We need to understand when operators want to share work, who can commit a driver and vehicle, and what should stay under their own control.

03 / A reason for guests to use it

Being online is a start.
Being useful is the reason to return.

We need both sides: operators who can deliver the service, and guests who have a reason to find and choose it.

FIND / CHOOSE / RETURN

Which guest?
Which need?
Why this service?

One clearly defined problem is a better starting point than trying to offer everything to every visitor.

  1. Find it

    Give the right people a way to discover it.

    Search and business listings, accommodation referrals, existing contacts, or carefully tested advertising are possible routes. A Google Business Profile is one option to investigate where eligible, not a guarantee of inquiries.

  2. Choose it

    Make the benefit easy to understand.

    A local ride, an arrival pickup, or a return already arranged. Start with a need operators can reliably meet, and explain what is clearer or easier than the alternatives.

  3. Use it again

    Deliver what the guest was told to expect.

    Accurate information and a dependable ride give the guest a reason to come back or recommend it. The website alone cannot create that experience.

These are routes to test, not a completed marketing plan. Advertising costs, demand, competing services and the effort needed to win a booking still need checking.

04 / Why ETA?

A familiar name.
A useful double meaning.

The same three letters connect the guest's journey with the work that makes it happen.

For operators

Enhanced Transportation Administrators

A name for the purpose behind the product: supporting the people who organize transportation. Clearer requests, easier coordination, and less duplicated administration around the work they already do.

For guests

Estimated Time of Arrival

Familiar transportation language, connected to a practical question: when will my ride arrive? It gives the name a recognizable link to pickups, departures, and getting where you need to be.

ETA is our working name for this prototype. The idea is to make the connection easy to understand; final branding still needs its own review.

06 / Learn enough to choose a small test

Start with understanding.
Not a sales pitch.

We need to learn what is actually happening before deciding what the service should change. Feedback is useful even when it tells us our idea is wrong.

Check what we can ourselves

Do the background work.

Existing booking and dispatch services already cover part of this ground. Our opportunity depends on doing something meaningfully better in practice, not assuming operators have no tools today.

Current pickup permissions, service classifications, fares and any separate platform charges still need verification before a live test. A medallion count is not a count of active businesses or potential paying accounts.

Ask what only operators can tell us

Listen to the working reality.

The work they complete, what they receive, and what they spend it on. Which bookings reserve a shared seat or a private vehicle. Who commits the driver, updates availability across other booking channels, and handles a failed pickup.

We want existing spending to work harder, not assume another bill is the answer. A price list can support later modeling, but it does not tell us actual costs or prove a return. No private customer lists, account details or documents are needed.

  1. Listen

    Walk through a recent booking and hear from willing operators with different ways of working.

  2. Choose

    Pick one recurring problem, a specific guest need, and a small group able to try a different approach.

  3. Prepare

    Confirm pickup eligibility, driver commitments, applicable fares and who handles changes before trying a real trip.

  4. Test & review

    Compare completed work, failures, effort and return with the current approach. Identify what was replaced or improved, not just what the app added.
